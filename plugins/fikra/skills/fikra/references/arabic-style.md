# Arabic Style and Brand Voice

Use this reference when writing or reviewing Arabic, bilingual, dialectal, or brand-voice content.

The goal is natural Arabic communication, not literal translation from English.

## Supported Language Modes

### Professional Modern Standard Arabic

Use for:

- Professional and institutional accounts
- LinkedIn
- Educational and journalistic content
- Sensitive or high-credibility subjects

Write clearly and professionally without unnecessary complexity.

### Simplified Modern Standard Arabic

Use for:

- General audiences
- Instagram
- Short educational videos
- Small-business content

Interpret «عربية مبسطة»، «عربية واضحة»، «عربية سهلة» and «عربية مهنية وقريبة» as natural, easy-to-read simplified Modern Standard Arabic (فصحى مبسطة), not colloquial Arabic or a local dialect, unless the user explicitly requests a specific dialect. Use familiar vocabulary, short sentences and direct address while preserving standard grammar and phrasing. A «مهنية وقريبة» or friendly tone means approachable wording; it does not authorize a switch to dialect.

Do not use dialectal vocabulary or constructions such as «مو»، «وش»، «اللي»، «تاخذ» or «يجيك» in authored campaign copy unless the user explicitly requests the relevant dialect. These are examples, not an exhaustive word filter: assess the whole sentence's register and meaning. For example, replace «مو لازم تاخذ ساعة» with «لا تحتاج إلى ساعة كاملة للقراءة», and «وش الكتاب اللي تقرأه؟» with «ما الكتاب الذي تقرؤه؟». Preserve the intended message, not the dialectal sentence structure.

### Neutral Social-Media Arabic

Use a flexible form of Arabic that is understandable across Arab countries.

It may be less formal than standard editorial Arabic, but it must remain clear, respectful, and grammatically coherent.

This mode does not override an explicit request for simplified, clear, easy or professional-and-approachable Arabic. Social-media context alone does not authorize a local dialect.

### Light Gulf Arabic

Use only when the user explicitly requests Gulf Arabic. A UAE/Gulf location, local business, young audience or approachable tone is not a dialect request.

- Use broadly understandable Gulf expressions.
- Avoid excessive local vocabulary.
- Do not invent dialect expressions.
- Do not mix several Gulf dialects in one text.
- Prefer natural Modern Standard Arabic when confidence in the dialect is low.

## Natural Arabic Rules

- Write the meaning naturally rather than preserving English sentence structure.
- Avoid unnecessary passive constructions.
- Avoid overly long sentences.
- Use direct verbs and clear subjects.
- Keep the main idea visible.
- Use punctuation consistently.
- Do not overuse rhetorical questions.
- Avoid inflated expressions that add no meaning.
- Avoid repeating the same idea using synonyms.
- Do not add diacritics unless they prevent ambiguity or are requested.

## Meaning Clarity in Visual Copy

Review meaning separately from grammar, spelling and correct Arabic rendering. Give each slide or scene one clear, direct idea. Read its headline and body as a nontechnical viewer would, without the campaign explanation: can they identify the intended message and a concrete action? Replace generic or ambiguous phrases, unexplained terms, and wording that could imply the opposite of the intended message. Do not rely on an explanatory caption to rescue unclear on-image text.

## Common Problems to Avoid

Avoid:

- Literal translation from English.
- Artificial motivational language.
- Excessive superlatives.
- Empty introductions.
- Repetitive calls to action.
- Unnecessary English words when a clear Arabic expression exists.
- Awkward translation of familiar technical terms.
- Mixing formal Arabic and dialect without a clear reason.
- Treating all Arab audiences as culturally identical.

## Technical and Foreign Terms

When a foreign term is widely recognized:

- Keep the familiar term when translating it would confuse the audience.
- Explain it briefly on first use when necessary.
- Use one form consistently throughout the campaign.

For bilingual content, do not translate mechanically. Rebuild the sentence naturally in each language while preserving the same meaning and factual claims.

## Platform Adaptation

### Instagram and TikTok

- Use shorter spoken or visual sentences.
- Prioritize immediate clarity.
- Make on-screen text concise.
- Avoid language that sounds like a formal report unless requested.

### LinkedIn

- Use credible professional Arabic.
- Keep the voice human and readable.
- Avoid inflated corporate language.

### X

- Use precise, concentrated wording.
- Remove unnecessary introductions.
- Keep threads logically connected.

## Brand Voice Extraction

When the user supplies representative samples, extract:

- Level of formality
- Typical sentence length
- Preferred vocabulary
- Opening style
- Ending style
- Use of questions
- Use of emoji
- Degree of personal storytelling
- Degree of directness
- Words or phrases to preserve
- Words or phrases to avoid

Summarize the extracted voice before applying it when the task is large or when the user requests confirmation.

## Brand Voice Boundaries

- Preserve the user's message and position.
- Do not invent biography, experience, or achievements.
- Do not imitate another living creator exactly.
- Do not reproduce distinctive phrases from unrelated creators.
- Use examples to infer general characteristics, then create original content.
- If supplied samples conflict, ask which style should take priority.

## Tone Modes

### Professional

Clear, credible, and direct. Avoid stiffness and unnecessary corporate language.

### Friendly

Warm and accessible without becoming overly casual or childish. Keep the requested language register; warmth does not imply colloquial Arabic.

### Educational

Explain one idea progressively using examples or steps. Avoid lecturing the audience.

### Marketing-Oriented

Focus on real value, audience need, and a relevant action. Avoid unsupported promises and pressure tactics.

### Journalistic

Separate confirmed information, analysis, and opinion. Attribute claims when sources are available.

### Inspirational

Use concrete experience or insight. Avoid generic motivational phrases.

### Narrative

Use a clear beginning, development, and takeaway. Do not invent personal details.

### Constructively Bold

Present a defensible perspective that invites discussion without insult, provocation for its own sake, or misleading claims.

## Emoji

- Use emoji only when appropriate to the user, platform, and brand.
- Do not add emoji by default to professional or sensitive content.
- Avoid placing emoji in every paragraph.
- Never use emoji as a substitute for clear structure.

## Calls to Action

Make the call to action consistent with the objective.

Examples of functions:

- Invite a focused comment.
- Ask the audience to save a useful guide.
- Encourage sharing with a relevant person.
- Request a qualified inquiry.
- Direct the audience to verified additional information.

Avoid generic engagement bait and artificial urgency.

## Arabic Quality Review

Before delivery, verify:

- Read every final headline, on-image/on-screen body, CTA, caption and any spoken script, separately from the explanation. All must use the requested register; a standard-Arabic explanation does not compensate for dialectal asset copy.
- For simplified/clear/easy/professional-and-approachable Arabic without an explicit dialect request, rewrite any colloquial vocabulary or sentence construction into natural simplified Modern Standard Arabic before presenting the text for approval. Check meaning and fluency, not just the example words above.
- The text reads naturally when spoken.
- The selected level of formality is consistent.
- The dialect is accurate and necessary.
- The meaning is not distorted by translation.
- Technical terms are consistent.
- The language suits the platform and audience.
- The call to action sounds natural.
- No invented cultural assumption appears.
